Practical answers before you buy software.

These guides are designed to help a small business owner understand the problem first, then decide what kind of tool is actually needed.

CRM

What is a CRM, and does a small business need one?

A CRM can centralize contacts, leads, notes, and follow-ups. The useful question is whether scattered customer information is already costing you time or missed follow-ups.

Start here: make a list of the customer information you currently track in email, spreadsheets, messages, and notebooks.

Ecommerce

Do you need an online store or just a website?

A service business may only need pages, contact details, and a way to book or request a quote. A product business may need catalog, cart, checkout, taxes, and shipping.

Start here: decide whether customers need to purchase products directly on the site.

Marketing

What should a small business automate first?

Start with repetitive work that happens often and has a clear trigger, such as a new lead, appointment request, or customer follow-up.

Start here: write down the three repetitive tasks you do most often each week, then look for a tool that handles one of them well.

See an official marketing example
A simple rule

Don't buy software to solve a problem you haven't defined.

Describe the task, how often it happens, and what “better” would look like. That makes it much easier to compare tools honestly.
